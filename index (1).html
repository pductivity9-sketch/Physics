Youth Activism & Climate Justice

YouthRising

How young people are leading the fight for climate justice.

Climate change affects everyone, but not everyone is affected equally. Around the world, young people are organizing, striking, educating, suing, and pressuring leaders to act.

A guided story in six parts
01 / The Problem

Climate change is not equal.

Climate justice means putting fairness, human rights, and future generations at the center of climate decisions.

The people who contributed least to climate change are often harmed the most.

Source: UNDP, 2023

3.3–3.6billion

people live in contexts highly vulnerable to climate change.

Source: IPCC, 2022

Climate risks are highest where poverty, weak infrastructure, and inequality overlap.

Source: IPCC, 2022

02 / Unequal Impact

Who pays the price?

Climate change does not hit every community the same way.

03 / The Solution

Climate justice means solving the crisis fairly.

Real solutions must work at individual, community, and government levels.

Individual

  • Learn about climate justice.
  • Share credible information.
  • Reduce waste where possible.
  • Support youth-led movements.
  • Vote when eligible.
  • Speak up in school and community spaces.

Community

  • Start or join school climate clubs.
  • Organize cleanups or awareness campaigns.
  • Push for greener schools.
  • Support local climate adaptation projects.
  • Build community education around climate justice.

Government

  • Clean energy transition.
  • Green jobs.
  • Fair disaster relief.
  • Green infrastructure.
  • Protection for vulnerable communities.
  • Youth representation in decision-making.
  • Climate policy based on science.

Sources: Fridays for Future, “Our Demands”; Sunrise Movement, “Green New Deal”; Sunrise Movement, “About”

04 / People

Solutions do not happen by themselves.

Young people turn climate problems into pressure.

05 / Movements

From individual voices to global movements.

Youth activism grows when personal action becomes organized pressure.

Fridays for Future

  • Started from Greta Thunberg’s 2018 school strike.
  • Youth-led global climate strike movement.
  • Uses school strikes, global protest days, and digital campaigns.
  • Pushes leaders toward science-based climate action.

Sources: Fridays for Future, “Who We Are”; Fridays for Future, “Our Demands”

Sunrise Movement

  • U.S.-based youth climate movement.
  • Connects climate action with economic and racial justice.
  • Pushes for Green New Deal-scale policy.
  • Uses direct action, electoral organizing, and movement-building.

Sources: Sunrise Movement, “About”; Sunrise Movement, “Green New Deal”

About

About This Project

This project explores how young people are leading the climate justice movement. Instead of treating climate change only as an environmental issue, it shows how climate change connects to inequality, human rights, race, income, geography, Indigenous rights, and future generations. Through youth activists and movements, the project shows how young people use protest, policy, education, digital activism, lawsuits, and storytelling to demand change.
